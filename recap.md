**PLAYOFFS WEEK 3 FINAL: COREY RUNS AWAY WITH IT, WILL GOES OUT ON THE BEST PITCHING WEEK ANYBODY HAD**

Three weeks, three eliminations, and the last one hurt. Corey closed the knockout rounds the way he opened them — as the best team in the field by a distance. Will threw the two finest starts of the entire Sunday and went home anyway.

**WEEK 3 ALONE** (of 64) — Corey 45.5 · Bryce 33.0 · Sawyer 30.0 · Tory 26.5 · Will 25.0

**Landen Poop — 45.5**
Five outright category leads: runs (50), homers (12), average (.300), OPS (.842) and innings (77), plus a share of the fewest walks allowed (21). Bobby Witt Jr. went 10-for-27 with eight runs, two homers, five driven in and three steals. Yandy Diaz added 10-for-27 with seven runs and two homers, Luis Arraez hit .476, and Luis Robert Jr. hit three homers in sixteen at-bats. Walker Buehler gave him twelve innings of three-run ball with eight strikeouts across two starts. He finishes the knockout rounds 16.5 clear of the field.

**Nick Kurtz in Paris — 26.5**
Second overall on the strength of Weeks 1 and 2, because this was only his fourth-best week of the five teams. The bullpen carried it: **12.0 in saves-plus-holds, double the next-best team**, and the league's best staff ERA at 3.15. Pete Alonso hit three homers going 10-for-29 and Kevin McGonigle drove in eight. But the bats finished last in OPS (.665), last in runs (29) and struck out the most (77). He is through, and he is through on pitching alone.

**hubba hubba bubba bubba — 33.0**
The most complete week of the four survivors. He led RBI (40), steals (9) and quality starts (5). **Jordan Walker led every hitter in the field in both hits and runs** — 12-for-34 with nine runs, three homers, eight RBI and two steals. Willson Contreras added two homers and six driven in, and Shane Baz struck out thirteen in twelve innings. The bill: a .231 average, worst in the field, and the most walks allowed (25).

**Chase Sapphire — 30.0, survives**
Third on the week, and that is exactly what saved him. He won the volume categories — 70 strikeouts, most in the field, and his own bats struck out the fewest times (56). Grant Taylor punched out eight in four relief innings with a save, Michael Wacha gave twelve innings and nine strikeouts, and Carson Benge went 9-for-24 with two homers. He was also last in RBI (31) with the worst ERA in the field (4.45). He survives by nine points on a week his rotation contributed almost nothing on the final day.

**Yordan Redemption Tour — 25.0, eliminated**
Read this and then look at where he finished. **Kyle Leahy threw nine innings across two starts without allowing an earned run and struck out ten. Nolan McLean threw twelve innings and gave up one earned run.** Nobody in this league had a better pair of starts all week. Will allowed the fewest hits in the field, 47, and the next-closest team was ten behind. Max Muncy hit .389 with two homers and seven RBI, Sal Stewart drove in seven.

And he is out, for two reasons. McLean's twelve innings came with **eight walks**, which dropped Will from first in fewest walks allowed to a tie for third — and cost him three and a half points in that one category on Sunday afternoon alone. The other reason is simpler and was true all week: 53 innings and 47 strikeouts, both last in the field by a wide margin. You cannot win an all-play format on efficiency when four other teams are outworking you two to one.
