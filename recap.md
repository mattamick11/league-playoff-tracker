**THE CHAMPIONSHIP: FOUR TEAMS, ZERO POINTS, FOURTEEN DAYS**

Aug 24 through Sep 6. **Landen Poop, Nick Kurtz in Paris, hubba hubba bubba bubba and Chase Sapphire.** Everything resets to zero — Corey's sixteen-point cushion, Tory's second seed, all of it. Three weeks of work bought these four an entry ticket and nothing else.

**HOW IT WORKS**
The whole fourteen days score as **one single combined block**, not two weeks. Four teams, three opponents each, sixteen categories: **48 decisions per team.** Six of those are already spent — cycles and no-hitters tie for everybody every time, so each of you banks 3.0 before a pitch is thrown and the actual fight is over 42.

There are four roster periods inside the block — Aug 24-27, Aug 28-30, Aug 31-Sep 3, and Sep 4-6 — so you get four chances to reset a lineup instead of the one you got each knockout week. Waivers matter more here than they have all month.

**WHY THIS IS A DIFFERENT GAME**
In a one-week round, a single hot series can carry a rate category. Over fourteen days it can't. **Batting average, OPS and ERA all converge toward what your roster actually is** — there is no hiding behind a lucky three-day sample, and no digging out of a bad one with one good Saturday.

Everything else roughly doubles. Innings, strikeouts, quality starts, saves-plus-holds, hits and walks allowed — those are accumulation categories, and eight or nine of the fourteen live ones reward simply having more arms take the mound. This block favors depth over talent, and it punishes an idle bench harder than any week so far.

**LANDEN POOP — the favorite, and it isn't close**
Corey led five categories outright in Week 3: runs, homers, average, OPS and innings. He is the only team here with an offense that wins rate categories *and* a staff that wins volume categories, which is a nasty combination when both halves of the board matter. His one soft spot is hits allowed — he gave up the most in the field (64) while throwing the most innings. If anybody beats him it will be by out-pitching him in the ratio categories, not by outscoring him.

**NICK KURTZ IN PARIS — the specialist**
Tory owns two categories almost by default: a 3.15 ERA and **12.0 in saves-plus-holds, double anyone else's.** In a fourteen-day block those hold up, and SVH3 in particular is the one category nobody else is even contesting. The problem is the other half of his roster. He finished Week 3 last in OPS, last in runs and with the most strikeouts at the plate — and over two weeks those don't regress, they compound. He needs his bats to be merely bad instead of historically bad.

**HUBBA HUBBA BUBBA BUBBA — the balanced one**
Bryce led RBI, steals and quality starts in Week 3, and quality starts is the tell: five in a week means rotation depth, and rotation depth is worth double here. He has the most obvious path to the volume categories other than Corey. What he has to fix is the batting average — .231 was the worst in the field — and the walks, where he allowed more than anyone. Balanced is good; balanced with two holes at the bottom of the board is how you finish third.

**CHASE SAPPHIRE — the one who shouldn't be here**
Sawyer was 9.0 points from elimination and got in on strikeouts and volume: 70 punchouts, most in the field, and the second-most innings behind Corey. That is exactly the profile that scales into a two-week block — strikeouts are the single most accumulable thing on this board. He also carried the worst ERA in the field (4.45) and finished last in RBI. He is the longest shot and also the one nobody should be surprised by anymore.

**WHAT TO WATCH**
Whether Tory's bullpen edge survives contact with a fourteen-day sample, and whether Corey's offense is as unbeatable over two weeks as it was over three days. First pitch is tomorrow. Nobody has a single point.
