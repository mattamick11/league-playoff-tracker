**Tuesday playoff recap — 15 games, and the all-play grid finally stretched out.** Monday left 220 ties across the field; Tuesday cut that to 132. The snapshot: Corey 63-15-18, Ryan 46-31-19, Bryce 41-37-18, Sawyer 40-40-16, Will 35-40-21, Tory 29-44-23 and Keller 16-63-17. Will and Bryce are protected from Week 1 elimination, but every result still carries forward.

**Corey / Landen Poop — 63-15-18.** Two days in, Corey isn’t merely leading—he’s dominating. It would take a major reversal over the final five days for him to tumble all the way to the Week 1 cut line. His offense went 16-for-38 (.421) with three homers and 13 RBI; seven hitters drove in a run, led by Mickey Moniak’s homer and three RBI. Brady Singer and Emerson Hancock each went 6 IP, 3 ER and 6 K, giving Corey two quality starts. The long-term news is much less comfortable: James Wood went onto the 10-day IL with a left oblique strain. Bryce Eldridge has also been managing a sore thumb, although he pinch-hit a two-run single Tuesday, while Bobby Witt Jr. returned from the IL.

**Ryan / Small Balls — 46-31-19.** Tarik Skubal’s first Dodgers outing since the trade: 6 IP, 4 H, 2 ER, 2 BB and 6 K at Wrigley, good for a quality start and 4.5 current all-play points. No Tuesday starter changed more of the grid: removing Skubal flips 14 of Ryan’s 96 comparisons. Nolan Arenado and Andrew Vaughn homered, while Jake McCarthy added three hits and two runs.

**Bryce / hubba hubba bubba bubba — 41-37-18.** Bryce made Tuesday’s biggest climb, from 38.0 record points to 50.0. His offense delivered four homers and 12 RBI: Liam Hicks went 2-for-5 with a homer and four RBI, and Nico Hoerner, Gleyber Torres and Jazz Chisholm Jr. also went deep. Joe Ryan labored through 5.1 IP and 4 ER, but David Bednar and Emilio Pagán each saved one. Jordan Walker was in the original lineup but was scratched before first pitch and never entered the game; there was no IL move or confirmed diagnosis as of Tuesday night.

**Sawyer / Chase Sapphire — 40-40-16.** Willi Castro and Carson Benge homered, while Brent Headrick’s one-inning hold was Sawyer’s entire pitching day. That is just scheduling, not danger: Sawyer used no starter Tuesday and still has plenty coming later in the week.

**Will / Yordan Redemption Tour — 35-40-21.** Grant Holmes delivered Tuesday’s only scoreless start: six innings, five hits, one walk and five strikeouts for a quality start. Trevor Megill added a save. The bats gave most of it back, going 9-for-48 (.188) with no homers; Jac Caglianone’s two hits, steal and RBI were the best of the group.

**Tory / Nick Kurtz in Paris — 29-44-23.** Wilyer Abreu homered twice, while Logan Henderson gave Tory 6 IP, 4 H, 2 ER, 1 BB and 8 K for a quality start. Bryan Baker added a clean two-strikeout save. Tory climbed from 35.0 to 40.5 snapshot points, but his namesake became another major injury story: Nick Kurtz went onto the 10-day IL, retroactive to August 1, with a strained right thumb.

**Keller / Return of Belt to Ass — 16-63-17.** This was a brutal split-screen. Keller’s hitters went 14-for-46 (.304) with 10 RBI; Manny Machado was 3-for-5 with four RBI, and Jackson Chourio went 3-for-4 with a homer. Then Freddy Peralta reached Coors: 3.2 IP, 9 H and 7 ER. Hogan Harris and Raisel Iglesias added four more earned runs in 1.2 innings, leaving Keller’s Tuesday staff at 5.1 IP and 11 ER and his week ERA at 12.10. He holds the lowest eligible snapshot, although the innings volume will recover quickly; the ERA damage is the part that is already real.

**The bench bit back.** Three playoff teams left quality starts in reserve Tuesday. Will sat Hayden Wesneski’s 6 IP, 4 H, 2 ER, 2 BB and 4 K against Toronto. Bryce sat J.T. Ginn’s 6 IP, 5 H, 3 ER, 2 BB and 5 K at Cincinnati. Tory took the biggest hit, leaving MacKenzie Gore’s scoreless six innings, three hits, three walks and four strikeouts on the bench against San Francisco. Some of that was defensible matchup risk—especially Ginn in Cincinnati—but the results still hurt. Simply adding those unused lines to the current grid would improve Will and Bryce by 3.0 record points apiece and Tory by 4.5.

Five days remain, and everyone is still one good—or ugly—night from reshuffling the board.
