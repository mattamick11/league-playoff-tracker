**PLAYOFFS WEEK 1: COREY TAKES THE WEEK, KELLER GOES HOME**

The first seven-team all-play week is official. Corey put together the league's most complete performance, Sawyer finished only 1.5 points behind him, and the defending champion is out. Keller never escaped the bottom after a disastrous pitching week and becomes the first team eliminated. The top-seed protection never actually came into play for Will—but that shield is gone now, and every Week 1 result carries forward.

**FINAL WEEK 1 BOARD**
1. Landen Poop — 55-28-13, **61.5 pts**
2. Chase Sapphire — 51-27-18, **60.0**
3. hubba hubba bubba bubba — 49-30-17, **57.5**
4. Nick Kurtz in Paris — 45-37-14, **52.0**
5. Small Balls — 36-46-14, **43.0**
6. Yordan Redemption Tour — 29-50-17, **37.5**
7. Return of Belt to Ass — 17-64-15, **24.5 — ELIMINATED**

**Landen Poop — 61.5**
Corey won Week 1 by attacking the counting categories with overwhelming volume: playoff highs in HR (13), innings (74⅓), strikeouts (77) and quality starts (6). Alec Burleson supplied four homers and seven RBI, while Kyle Tucker hit .364 with a 1.144 OPS. The pitching depth was absurd: Payton Tolle struck out 14 in six innings, Noah Cameron allowed one run across eight, and Ian Seymour added another six-inning, one-run quality start. Corey actually allowed the most hits in the field (73), but he generated so much IP, K and QS that it barely mattered. The only cloud is James Wood's oblique injury, a major blow to Corey's ceiling beyond Week 1. Two dominant days turned into a wire-to-wire win.

**Chase Sapphire — 60.0**
Sawyer missed the top spot by only 1.5 points and owned the league's best offense: first in runs (43), AVG (.266) and OPS (.843). Carson Benge went 9-for-22 with six runs, two homers and six RBI; Bryson Stott somehow went 10-for-22 with three steals and **zero strikeouts**. Junior Caminero added three bombs. The staff had some early land mines, but Drew Rasmussen and Sonny Gray combined for 13 scoreless innings and 16 strikeouts, and Sawyer allowed a playoff-low 40 hits. This was not merely a protected-seed coast—Sawyer nearly won the entire week.

**hubba hubba bubba bubba — 57.5**
Bryce paired the best team ERA (2.71) with the fewest hitter strikeouts (54), then backed it with 63 innings and five quality starts. Ronald Acuña Jr. hit three homers, Griffin Conine posted a 1.326 OPS, and Liam Hicks drove in six without striking out once. On the mound, Sandy Alcantara threw seven scoreless, Shane Baz delivered eight innings of two-run ball, and Cam Schlittler struck out 17 over two starts. There was no single runaway offensive category here—just an extremely clean, balanced week that left Bryce safely in third.

**Nick Kurtz in Paris — 52.0**
Tory survived the Nick Kurtz thumb injury because Wilyer Abreu temporarily turned into Barry Bonds: 7-for-16, seven runs, three homers, nine RBI, one steal and a 1.584 OPS. Chandler Simpson added 11 hits and four steals, while Pete Alonso supplied two homers and six RBI. Tory also led the field with eight saves/holds: Bryan Baker and Riley O'Brien earned three saves apiece, and Yoendrys Gómez added two more. Five quality starts steadied the rotation; Justin Wrobleski's 10 earned runs were the main reason Tory didn't finish closer to the leaders.

**Small Balls — 43.0**
Ryan had two of the best individual performances in the league. Jake McCarthy went an outrageous 14-for-27 with eight runs, two homers, five RBI and three steals, while Logan Webb authored the pitching line of the week: 14 innings, one earned run, two quality starts and a 0.64 ERA. Tarik Skubal added a quality start in his first outing for Ryan after the Dodgers trade. The team finished second in runs (42) and AVG (.264), but a few rough starts and only 1.5 saves/holds dragged down the pitching board. Ryan advances, but only 5.5 points separate him from Will entering Week 2.

**Yordan Redemption Tour — 37.5**
Will never needed his top-seed protection in the end—Keller finished 13 points behind him—but this was still the elimination danger zone. Jac Caglianone was spectacular—11-for-23 with two homers, seven RBI and a 1.348 OPS—and Gabriel Moreno hit .368. Trevor Megill was perfect over four innings and collected three saves; Jacob Webb added two more. But Zach Neto, Ketel Marte and Max Muncy combined for just seven hits, and the offense finished with only eight homers and two steals. The protection has now expired, so Will begins Week 2 as the lowest surviving team, 5.5 points behind Ryan.

**Return of Belt to Ass — 24.5, eliminated**
Keller found several genuinely great performances. Jackson Chourio hit four homers, Ángel Martínez drove in nine, Dylan Cease threw seven scoreless with 10 strikeouts, and Bryce Elder allowed one hit over seven innings. It still wasn't remotely enough. Casey Mize, Freddy Peralta, Kumar Rocker and Raisel Iglesias combined to allow **25 earned runs and 31 hits in 12⅔ innings**. Keller finished last in runs, homers, RBI, AVG and OPS, then posted a 6.55 ERA. The reigning champion's title defense ends after one week.

**THE LOSERS BRACKET — ABSOLUTE AGONY**
Burz escaped by half a point, **40.0-39.5**, over Matt. Sunday night made the margin almost comically literal: Fernando Tatis Jr. hit a three-run homer and later doubled, but Gavin Sheets answered with the solo homer that tied Cousin in HR and supplied Burz's winning half-point. One more Tatis RBI would have pushed Matt ahead; it never came. Burz is safely out of the losers bracket, while Matt, Cousin, Con and Jack continue next week.
